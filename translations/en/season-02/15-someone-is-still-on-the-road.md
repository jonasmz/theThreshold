# Echoes of the Threshold
## Season 2 · Episode 15: Someone Is Still on the Road

**Status:** Narrative draft 0.1. **Point of view:** Lyra Saren, limited third person.

***

The mail driver returned to the docks without his cart.

Lyra Saren saw him come in through the rain, coat hanging open, a small wheel cradled in his arms. He was the same man who had kept his route book when an office with no verifiable authority tried to collect the receipts from a technical inquiry. This time, he wasn't carrying another demand.

“My assistant's still out on the old bridge road,” he said. “The cart lost a supporting axle. There's a box of supplies for the dispensary, and we can't get it back to storage before closing.”

Ema set her accounts aside.

“Is anyone hurt?”

“No. He's sheltered at the guard post. But the alternative transport route won't reach him until tomorrow. And the storage facility refuses to extend the refrigeration charges another night.”

Lyra looked at the wheel. The wood had split around the axle housing.

“I can't fix that with a rope,” she said.

“I'm not asking for a miracle. They told me you know the haulers along the embankment. I need someone to bring a light cart, check the shipment, and get back before the shift changes.”

Ema showed her the regular supply schedule. There was a window available for delivering medicine to the dispensary, but it had already been allocated to another shipment. Giving it away without agreement would displace people who were waiting, too.

“I'm not taking that vehicle away from them,” Lyra said.

The driver pressed the wheel against his chest.

“Then I don't know what to do.”

Lyra remembered her medical restrictions: she could perform local work and take rest breaks, but she had not been cleared for prolonged journeys or Transit operations. The problem was a few hours away by ordinary road, not on the other side of a Threshold. Even so, taking on the entire journey herself would be another way to disregard a limit because something felt urgent.

“We'll find an authorized relief crew,” she said. “And this time no one travels without knowing who's paying for the return trip.”

***

In the yard, Lyra found a hauler willing to move an empty cart if Ema could guarantee the fuel.

Ema shook her head.

“I won't spend the shelter's reserve on a service commissioned by another office. I can help you find a quote and arrange a receiving window. I can't give away resources belonging to people who aren't here to defend them.”

The driver offered his week's wages. It wasn't enough.

Lyra calculated the distance and vehicle capacity. She requested two quotes and chose one that allowed a sealed medical container to be carried without opening it or disturbing its seals. The storage facility would have to confirm that the unit was suitable for transport and that the dispensary would accept it during the revised window.

“Why are you getting involved again?” Ema asked as Lyra prepared the messages.

Lyra recognized the question.

“Because his assistant is waiting, and nobody's told him whether he can leave his post. I don't want his decision to depend on a promise no one made.”

“That doesn't mean you have to pay for everything.”

“I'm not going to.”

The haulers' association agreed to advance part of the expense as assistance to a member, conditional on a written incident report. The medical storage facility offered to inspect the container at the handover point, but would not accept responsibility for transport without a supplementary contract. The driver signed for the remaining cost.

Lyra was listed as the coordinator of communications, not the owner of the cargo.

When the vehicle departed, it carried an order naming three responsible parties and a simple rule: if the container was not within the required temperature range and properly sealed, it would not be transported as usable medication. Health would decide what happened next.

It wasn't a secret route.

It was an expensive, slow operation that anyone authorized could trace.

***

Lyra traveled only as far as the nearby relief station, where drivers could change without prolonging her own journey. There she received the first message from the road.

The assistant had managed to keep the container in a ventilated shed. He hadn't opened the box. The packaging's temperature strip showed an interval that needed verification by the medical recipient; the young man had no authority to interpret it as either safe or spoiled.

“Can he leave the post?” Lyra asked over the communicator.

The response took time.

“He won't go without someone signing for the shipment,” the relief driver reported. “He says they'll make him pay for the box afterward.”

Lyra asked to speak to him.

His voice was younger than she had expected.

“If I leave, who's going to prove I left it sealed?” the assistant asked.

“You can demand an inventory and a receipt. You don't have to stay until you're sick just to prove you've done your job.”

“The boss said he'd come.”

“He's trying to get there, but the cart is broken. Nobody can guarantee he'll see the shipment today. That's why we've sent authorized relief.”

The young man fell silent.

Then he asked something else.

“Did you see who came asking for our route book?”

Lyra felt the urge to answer with more than she knew.

“I saw a request that didn't demonstrate authorization. I didn't see the person who delivered it, and I can't be sure why it was sent.”

“The man who talked to me at the toll station said there'd be trouble with our contracts if we kept copies.”

“When did he say that?”

The assistant explained that it had happened before the breakdown, during a routine stop. He didn't know the man's name or whether he worked for the same office. Lyra asked him not to force himself to identify someone under pressure.

“First take care of your safety and the delivery,” she said. “Afterward, if you want to report what you heard, we can ask the proper authority how to protect your information.”

“I don't want the boss losing his work because of me.”

“That isn't yours to decide alone, either.”

***

The relief crew reached the shed before nightfall.

Lyra listened to the confirmations from the nearby station: outer seal visually intact, temperature-strip reading recorded without interpretation, package number matching, and receipt by the new driver. The assistant signed only for the physical handover. He did not certify that the contents remained usable.

The vehicle departed for the dispensary, where an authorized professional would determine whether to accept the medication. The young man climbed into the empty passenger seat for the return to the docks.

Lyra returned to Vael-Tir by the shorter route, without traveling through the night. She had lost hours and money, but had used neither a Transit license nor the shelter's reserves.

When she arrived, the driver was waiting with a cup of soup in his hands. Ema had left an empty chair for the assistant.

“The dispensary received the container,” the man said. “It's being evaluated. If they reject the contents, there'll be a formal loss procedure.”

“That's how it should be,” Lyra replied.

The assistant came in, exhausted and soaked. Before sitting down, he laid his route notebook on the table.

“I want to tell someone what they said to me at the toll station,” he murmured. “But not in front of everyone on the docks.”

Lyra didn't take the notebook.

“You don't have to give it to me. We can find advice and somewhere that understands what protections you're entitled to.”

The young man regarded her suspiciously.

“What if nobody believes me?”

Lyra remembered the promise she had written in Selivar, a promise she would never again experience as a living memory.

“Then at least they'll have to tell you what they checked and what they couldn't. I'm not going to promise a statement will fix your job.”

The young man nodded.

That night, the help he had been waiting for had arrived. The cargo had changed hands without vanishing from the records.

Another wait remained: finding someone who could hear a threat without turning its witness into the next thing everyone wanted to move.

***End of Episode 15.***
