# The Tide of the Absent
## Chapter 055 · Under Oath

**Status:** First draft, English translation. **POV:** Breda Lin. **Location:** Port Cendra, EC 548. **Source:** `precuelas/la-marea-de-los-ausentes/capitulos/055-bajo-juramento.md`.

***

Breda Lin asked for the question to be read to her a second time.

The hearing had resumed after the blackout, with a different recorder and a technician assigned to certify it. Yuna Tor had ordered the internal clock checked, the medium's serial number recorded, and a backup copy preserved. No one wanted to discover afterward that the device had stopped working.

Breda had sworn to tell the truth. She did not need to be reminded; she needed to understand exactly what she was being asked.

“Did you identify four duplicate payments in the transport account?” the company's lawyer repeated.

Breda looked at the sheet in front of her.

“No. I identified four settlements that shared a single transport reference. In my first table, I used the word ‘duplicate’ as a provisional heading. It was an imprecise description.”

The lawyer leaned toward her.

“Are you withdrawing your accusation, then?”

“I didn't make an accusation. I'm withdrawing that word. We still don't know whether the common reference relates to a bundled service, a repeated invoice, or an administrative allocation that was never carried out.”

“Why didn't you correct it sooner?”

“I corrected it in the reconciliation report I submitted to the independent expert. I should also have pointed it out in the first version that circulated.”

The magistrate noted the answer. Breda had learned that a late correction could not be hidden behind a technical explanation.

***

Expert Sema Ordel sat beside the clerk. She had received the originals under limited custody and prepared a table separating five stages: order, dispatch, receipt, payment, and reconciliation.

Breda asked for the entire table to be displayed, not just the page containing the four references.

“An order shows that someone requested a service,” she explained. “A dispatch note may show that a load or vehicle left. A destination receipt establishes delivery if signed by an identifiable, authorized person. A bank or cash entry proves that a payment was made, but not necessarily that the service was performed. Reconciliation has to connect each stage.”

The lawyer asked whether a company could use a single reference for several jobs.

“Yes. A framework contract can cover different crews and routes. That doesn't make it irregular in itself.”

“Then why did you include all four entries in a discrepancy report?”

“Because the amounts were settled as completed services, but not all of them had individual receipts of delivery. The shared reference justified reviewing the group. It did not prove that four improper charges had been made.”

The answer did not satisfy the workers' representative, who had brought a folder of complaints. Breda saw him shake his head.

She understood the frustration. If every qualification delayed payment, the workers would keep waiting. But calling a discrepancy fraud when a bundled contract might explain it would make legitimate claims harder to distinguish from false ones.

“Can you say how many services have been substantiated?” Yuna asked.

“I can say how many have each type of supporting record. I won't give a single overall number until the outstanding confirmations are complete.”

***

The Mutual's lawyer stood with a copy of the collective certification from the previous autumn.

“Ms. Lin, you signed this document. Do you still maintain that the Mutual verified all seven vessels?”

Breda felt the question opening a door she had tried to close with an earlier statement.

“I maintain that my department reviewed the documentation campaign. I do not maintain that I personally inspected all seven vessels.”

“Why did you sign a certification that did not make that distinction?”

“Because I accepted a sample-based verification and a supplier's warranty. The final wording implied a broader inspection than the one actually carried out.”

“Were you misled by your director?”

“I can't claim that. He proposed proceeding with a global certification. I had professional responsibility for the wording I signed.”

“Did you conceal the limitations when the investigation began?”

“No. I stated them in my first appearance. Later, I asked for the transcript to be corrected when the summary omitted that distinction.”

The lawyer submitted the corrected copy. Yuna confirmed that the document was in the case file.

“Do you acknowledge that your work was negligent?”

Breda drew a slow breath.

“I acknowledge that my verification was insufficient for the breadth of the statement I signed. I cannot legally characterize my own conduct. I can explain what I did and what I failed to do.”

The Mutual's representative lowered his eyes. The answer did not absolve Breda or give the company a confession to extend to every document in the audit.

***

The prosecutor asked her to explain how her method had changed after reviewing the crews' receipts.

Breda showed a worksheet with three columns: confirmed, pending, and contradictory information. Each entry cited its supporting document and source; copies for public circulation concealed unnecessary personal details.

“Before, we used a single category called ‘settled,’” she said. “Now we distinguish between money advanced, money paid to an intermediary, and money received by the final recipient.”

“What difference did that make?”

“We found cases in which a foreman received envelopes to distribute, but there are no individual receipts. We can't say the workers were paid. We also can't claim the foreman kept the money without examining his accounts and the statements of those who received it.”

The prosecutor pointed to a row.

“Here is a payment recorded for a crew that doesn't appear on the official register.”

“The crew worked. We have three shift notebooks, two testimonies, and a tool receipt. The register omitted their names. That raises a labor and recordkeeping problem; it doesn't make the workers nonexistent.”

“And the money?”

“Part of it is documented as having been withdrawn by the foreman. The final distribution is still pending.”

The company's lawyer asked that a payroll omission not be confused with a financial crime.

“I agree,” Breda said. “They're related questions, but they're different.”

Yuna looked up.

“Why didn't you submit this table earlier?”

“It didn't exist when I signed the original report. We built it after obtaining receipts, interviewing workers, and comparing cashbooks. The new documentation lets us review the earlier record; it doesn't mean the material was always available.”

***

During the recess, Nimo approached Breda. He was no longer wearing the Mutual's badge; he had been transferred to an archive office while the files were being reviewed.

“Some workers say you're weakening the case,” he said.

“I'm separating what can be proved from what can't yet.”

“The company will use every correction to say there were no irregularities.”

“It would also use an exaggerated accusation to discredit the entire report.”

Nimo handed her a note. An employee had found the receipt for two sacks that had previously been listed as an unresolved discrepancy. The paper bore the dispensary's stamp and a date later than the original delivery.

Breda examined it.

“This reduces the discrepancy,” she said.

“Doesn't it increase the suspicion that someone corrected the records afterward?”

“Not necessarily. The receipt may have reached the archive late. We need to compare its ink, serial number, and date of entry. We can't call it false just because it answers a question.”

Nimo seemed disappointed.

“You used to be quicker at spotting patterns.”

“I used to confuse spotting a pattern with understanding it.”

She asked him to submit the document through the custody procedure, not directly to her. Their former working relationship could make an informal handover vulnerable to challenge. Nimo agreed and left.

Breda returned to the room with a copy of the notice, not the original receipt.

***

Yuna asked whether she wished to add anything before the statement was closed.

Breda thought of the autumn certification, the payments she had withheld, and the workers missing from the lists. She did not want to turn her oath into a theatrical confession.

“Yes,” she said. “I want it recorded that my mistakes do not automatically prove that every transaction was false. I signed a certification broader than what I had verified. It would also be a mistake to say that every discrepancy I found proves fraud. There are people who received assistance, people who worked without being registered, and people we still cannot locate. If we mix those categories, we'll erase again the very people we're trying to identify.”

The magistrate asked for the statement to be entered in full.

The Mutual's lawyer announced that he reserved the right to challenge the scope of the audit. The workers' counsel requested access to the individual appendices under privacy controls. Yuna ordered that separate copies be prepared for each party.

The hearing ended without an overall conclusion.

Breda signed the transcript after reading it line by line. On the final page, an editorial correction replaced “four duplicate payments” with “four settlements sharing a common reference.” This time, she did not let the wording pass.

As she left, a man from the workers' group approached.

“You didn't help us much today,” he said.

Breda did not try to persuade him.

“Maybe not.”

“But at least you didn't say we'd been paid when we don't have the receipt.”

She nodded.

The man put away his folder and walked toward the square.

Breda remained by the door. She had lost the comfort of appearing infallible, if she had ever possessed it. She had kept something more fragile: a method for correcting mistakes without erasing evidence that still needed examination.

She did not know whether that would be enough to preserve everyone's trust.

She knew it was the only thing she could swear to.
