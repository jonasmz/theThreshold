# The Tide of the Absent
## Chapter 057 · Fifteen Hours of Reserve

**Status:** First draft, English translation. **POV:** Neria Calden. **Location:** Port Cendra, EC 548. **Source:** `precuelas/la-marea-de-los-ausentes/capitulos/057-quince-horas-de-reserva.md`.

***

Neria Calden erased the number fifteen from the board and wrote it again.

The calculation had been simple the first time. The eastern station consumed a steady amount of energy under normal operating conditions, and the main accumulator could sustain essential instruments for an extended shift. But normal operating conditions no longer existed. The preservation order had added monitoring, redundant readings, and tests that could not be carried out with the installation connected to its usual grid.

Fifteen was not a promise. It was the provisional limit obtained by applying measured consumption to the available charge, with an allowance for performance loss caused by temperature.

Irsa set a cup beside the board.

“The authority wants to know whether we can close the auxiliary branch until the tests are finished.”

“We can isolate it. The question is how long we can maintain surveillance without that feeder.”

“They say the court order requires us to preserve it.”

“It requires us to preserve its condition and records. Not to keep energized a circuit that could interfere with the measurements.”

Neria pointed to the diagram. The auxiliary branch powered the secondary indicator, an inspection lamp, and a repeater that was no longer supposed to take part in hydraulic control. The station had a separate accumulator for critical sensors and the gate's safety circuit.

“If we disconnect the branch, we don't lose mechanical control,” she explained. “But we reduce the readings available and use reserve power while we verify that isolation hasn't changed anything.”

“How long?”

Neria looked at the fifteen again.

“Fifteen hours under measured conditions. No more.”

***

The test would be conducted with Amina Dorel present and a distribution technician assigned to check the accumulator's output. They could not drain it to its limit just to discover its capacity: if the voltage fell below the safety relay's minimum, the measurement itself would become an operational hazard.

Neria requested a stepped test. First, they would measure the idle load. Then they would connect a calibrated resistance bank to reproduce essential consumption, without activating motors or gates. They would record voltage, temperature, and the rate of decline at intervals. The final calculation would include the reserve the station itself needed to carry out a manual shutdown if the auxiliary circuit failed to respond.

The technician inspected the bank.

“It doesn't represent a pump's starting surge.”

“We're not starting a pump,” Neria said. “The gate is mechanically secured. This calculation is for sensors and control, not for moving water.”

Amina recorded the limitation in the report.

The test began at nine. Voltage declined gradually. Irsa logged each reading and compared it with the independent indicator. At the third hour, the accumulator remained within its operating margin. At the fifth, the casing temperature had risen by two degrees. The technician reduced the load to check whether ventilation was adequate.

Neria would not let anyone round the figures upward.

“If the margin is fifteen hours, we write fifteen as the theoretical maximum. For planning, we use twelve.”

“Why three hours less?” the port representative asked.

“Because we don't control the outside temperature, the aging of every cell, or the load an emergency might demand. A calculated reserve is not energy we are free to spend.”

***

At noon, an administrative request arrived. Inspecting the auxiliary branch required an eighteen-hour window, including disassembly, cleaning, replacement of insulators, and a continuity test. The contractor maintained that it could finish before the reserve fell to its minimum.

Neria read the schedule.

“No.”

The representative frowned.

“The company guarantees it will finish in eighteen hours.”

“The accumulator doesn't guarantee it will last eighteen hours.”

“The grid may come back sooner.”

“It may. But we cannot schedule a safety operation around the hope that it will.”

The man pointed to the court order.

“The magistrate requires us to preserve the circuit. If we don't work on it, we can't ensure it remains in proper condition.”

“We can preserve it and isolate it. What we cannot do is consume the entire reserve to finish a repair that can be divided into stages.”

Neria proposed splitting the work. During the first ten-hour window, the team would document and isolate the branch, replace only those insulators that would not alter the relay's configuration, and check the condition of the main connection. The remaining work would be done once external power was stable and a second backup source was available.

The contractor protested that splitting the job would increase the cost.

“The cost of a second visit is lower than losing surveillance of the gate,” Neria replied.

***

The discussion moved to the municipal hall. Orin had been summoned because partially closing the branch would require two cargo maneuvers to be rescheduled. The cooperative's representative did not want boats trapped in the channel if the station had to reduce its operations.

“I need a schedule I can give the captains,” he said. “I can't tell them there are fifteen hours of reserve and maybe nothing will happen.”

Neria drew a line on the plan.

“The main passage will remain open. The eastern gate will be secured in its service position, under manual surveillance and redundant readings. The auxiliary branch will be isolated. No opening maneuvers are scheduled during the window.”

“What if the level rises?”

“The duty crew has a manual emergency procedure and a separate reserve for the closing mechanism. If independent readings show an actual change, we'll stop the test and prioritize safety.”

Orin studied the plan.

“How long without maneuvers?”

“Ten hours planned. Twelve at most if a minor difficulty comes up. I won't accept eighteen.”

“Then the cooperative needs to move the grain barge before we start.”

“Yes.”

Orin asked for an hour's notice to organize the tow. He did not like changing the workload, but he preferred a defined route to an energy promise no one could keep.

***

At fifteen hours into the test, the accumulator had not reached its critical threshold, but the model showed little remaining margin for safe operation. Amina ordered the test stopped before a full discharge. Voltage stabilized when the resistance bank was removed.

Neria reviewed the results. The initial fifteen-hour estimate held under essential load and at the recorded temperature. However, the older cells showed greater variation. If the weakest cell was used as the limiting value, usable reserve could fall to thirteen and a half hours.

“Then it isn't fifteen,” Irsa said.

“It is fifteen for the measured assembly, thirteen and a half if we use the most degraded unit as the limit. For operations, twelve.”

Amina asked that the report distinguish all three figures: estimated maximum, conservative minimum, and authorized work window. The record would also state which capacities had not been tested: motor starts, pump operation, and repeated gate maneuvers.

The port representative accepted the ten-hour schedule on one condition: if the external grid returned to a stable state, a second window could be requested to complete the work.

Neria signed.

***

The operation began before dawn. Orin had moved the grain barge, and the cooperative kept the channel clear. Irsa photographed the connections before removing the branch cover. Amina checked the number of every tool and the condition of the seals.

Isolation was completed without incident. The secondary indicator went out of service, but the independent level sensor and manual log remained active. The gate did not move. Its position was secured with a mechanical lock and verified by two operators.

At the sixth hour, the technician found an insulator with an old crack. It was unsafe to reuse, but replacing it would affect a component relevant to the inspection. Amina authorized its removal after it had been photographed, measured, and packed with a custody label. The repair would be completed with a new reference component, while the original remained preserved.

The work ended at nine hours and twenty minutes. The reserve had been used less than expected because isolating the branch reduced the repeater's load. Neria did not interpret that result as a reason to extend future work windows.

“We had some margin today,” Irsa said.

“Today we measured before spending.”

Back in the office, Neria updated the maintenance board. She wrote: “Theoretical reserve: 15 hours. Conservative limit: 13.5. Authorized work window: 10. Do not plan to consume down to threshold.”

Below it, she added the names of the two operators who had verified the lock.

The number fifteen remained on the board.

It no longer looked like a countdown. It was a warning about the difference between what a machine could withstand in a test and what a city had the right to demand of it.
