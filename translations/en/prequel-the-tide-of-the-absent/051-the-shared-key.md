# The Tide of the Absent
## Chapter 051 · The Shared Key

**Status:** First draft, English translation. **POV:** Neria Calden. **Location:** Port Cendra, EC 548. **Source:** `precuelas/la-marea-de-los-ausentes/capitulos/051-la-llave-compartida.md`.

***

Neria Calden found the key inside an envelope bearing her name, although it did not belong to her.

The envelope had arrived at the maintenance office with a contradictory inspection notice. Mareval had appointed Edran Voss as its technical expert to examine the external adjustment on the eastern gauge. The municipality accepted the inspection, provided it took place in the presence of Amina Dorel, the court-appointed expert, and every operation was recorded.

Neria recognized Edran's handwriting before she read the signature.

He had been her supervisor for eight years. He had taught her to listen for a transmission that began to vibrate before the pressure gauge registered anything, and he had also taught her never to dismantle a component merely because someone in administration wanted a quick answer. He had retired from port management two years earlier, after a dispute over the preventive-maintenance budget. Since then, he had worked as an adviser to contractors.

The key in the envelope belonged to the external housing of the gauge. Its label bore the inventory number Neria had seen in the autumn register.

“Does he still have it?” Irsa asked.

“He shouldn't.”

“Are you going to hand it over to him?”

Neria put the key back in the envelope.

“I'm going to ask that the names of the person who brings it and the person who receives it be recorded. I'm not going to pretend it never existed.”

The inspection would be adversarial: each party could observe, ask questions, and keep a copy of the record. No one could operate the mechanism alone. Neria had agreed because the court should not have to rely solely on her account, however well she knew the system.

***

Edran arrived at the eastern station accompanied by a lawyer and an instrument technician. He had aged less than Neria had expected. His hair was still short, and he still carried a black notebook in the inside pocket of his coat.

“Calden,” he said in greeting.

“Voss.”

They did not shake hands.

Amina reviewed the conditions: photographs of the initial state, identification of tools, pressure isolation, independent measurement, and a prohibition on adjusting the zero until everyone had signed the record. If any component had to be removed, it would be stored in a numbered container.

Edran's technician presented a reference pressure gauge with a current certificate. Neria showed them the level column and the station's auxiliary meter. The three instruments did not measure precisely the same quantity, so Amina instructed everyone not to treat them as interchangeable.

“The reference gauge will be connected to the test port,” she explained. “The column will continue recording the actual water level. The suspect indicator will remain immobilized until its position has been documented.”

Edran examined the order.

“The service key is missing.”

Neria took out the envelope.

“It arrived this morning addressed to me.”

He recognized the label.

“A copy from my office.”

“Why do you have it?”

“Because I didn't return all the calibration tools when I left management. The outer housing was considered shared-access equipment for inspections. It's in the inventory.”

“It isn't on the current key register.”

Edran did not argue.

Amina asked that the key remain on the table until its number had been checked and photographed. Mareval's lawyer requested that the record state that possession alone did not establish recent use. The expert accepted the observation.

***

The outer housing stood in the storeroom adjoining the control room. Its cover had two independent locks: one municipal and one for service. The first remained in Neria's custody; the second required the key Edran had presented. The system had been designed so that no one could adjust the indicator without the participation of both functions.

“An old custom,” Edran said. “The service key was never supposed to leave the office.”

“But it did.”

“Yes.”

Neria inserted the municipal key. Edran waited while Amina recorded the number on his key before opening the second lock. They turned both keys at the same time. The cover gave way with a dry click.

Inside lay dust, a short shaft, and an adjustment wheel. There was no visible power source or mechanism capable of moving the gate. It was a purely mechanical system, connected to the indicator by a rod passing through the wall.

Amina took photographs from four angles. Edran's technician measured the wheel's travel without turning it. Neria noticed a red paint mark on the retaining screw.

“The mark is broken,” she said.

“It may have worn away,” Edran replied.

“Or the screw may have turned.”

“That doesn't establish when it happened or who did it.”

Neria looked at him. It was exactly the answer she herself would have given.

The reference gauge was connected to the test port after the section had been isolated. The operator opened the calibration valve in small increments. The main indicator responded with a three-division offset; the outer column remained steady because the water level had not been changed.

The test confirmed that the external adjustment could alter the reading without changing the actual hydraulic level. It did not prove that anyone had used it during the flood.

Edran asked to repeat the test with the reference gauge in the opposite direction. Amina authorized a second series after confirming that the first had returned to zero. The result was equivalent, within the instrument's margin of error.

***

While reviewing the maintenance log, Neria found the order from the previous autumn. Edran's signature authorized an external calibration. The personnel register showed that he had been on leave on the dates when the intervention was recorded as completed.

“Did you sign before you left?” she asked.

Edran read the page.

“I authorized the work and left instructions. I don't remember who carried it out.”

“The form makes it look as though you supervised the completion.”

“Then the form is badly worded.”

“Or you signed off on work you hadn't witnessed.”

Edran did not answer immediately.

“I may have signed an open work order that was later closed under my authorization signature,” he said. “I shouldn't have allowed the form to confuse authorization with acceptance.”

Amina asked for the statement to be included as an observation, not a conclusion. The signature could be compared with other samples, but handwriting alone would not establish who had performed the task.

Neria remembered that he had taught her never to confuse a component with the report describing it. She also remembered how often she had defended his methods to workers who questioned his rigidity.

“Did you know the key was still active?” she asked.

“Yes. I didn't think anyone had kept it without registering it.”

“You kept it.”

“And I should have returned it.”

It was not a confession of tampering. It was an acknowledgment of a failure of control.

***

The inspection ended with a six-page record. The photographs, measurements, reference-gauge certificate, and each party's observations were attached. The main indicator was immobilized; the outer housing was closed with new numbered seals, one municipal and one judicial. The service key was handed to Amina against a receipt.

Edran asked for a copy of the record.

“You'll have one when it's certified,” Neria said.

“Don't you trust my copy?”

“I trust every copy to have an origin.”

The former supervisor put away his notebook.

“You still ask questions as though the answer could fix something.”

“No. I ask them so people won't keep calling anything a repair.”

Edran left without saying goodbye.

Irsa gathered the tools. The test had required a forty-minute pause in the monitoring schedule and a small additional draw from the calibration accumulator. No gate had been operated, and the distribution network had not been altered.

Neria signed off on the shift. In the conclusions field, she wrote that the external adjustment could change the reading without changing the hydraulic level, but that the test did not establish the date, author, or purpose of the earlier intervention.

The shared key remained in judicial custody.

Before closing the office, Neria received a call from Vela. The signal operator had been summoned to testify and wanted to know whether the inspection had produced a conclusive result.

“It produced a measurable result,” Neria said. “Not the one some people are expecting.”

“That counts too.”

Neria looked at the empty space where the service key used to be kept.

For the first time, the space did not look like a loss. It was proof that no one should ever open that housing again without another person being able to see it.
