# The Tide of the Absent
## Chapter 060 · The Empty Hearing

**Status:** First draft, English translation. **POV:** Amiel Rusk. **Location:** Port Cendra, EC 548. **Source:** `precuelas/la-marea-de-los-ausentes/capitulos/060-la-audiencia-vacia.md`.

***

The chair reserved for Edran Voss remained empty when Yuna Tor opened the hearing.

Amiel Rusk had arrived before the others. On the table lay the autumn calibration order, the key register, and photographs of the eastern station's outer housing. The appearance was meant to clarify three points: who had received the service key, who had carried out the intervention recorded while Edran was on leave, and why the completion form bore a signature that could be mistaken for acceptance of the work.

This was not the final hearing. The magistrate had to decide whether the testimony and documents justified expanding the inquiry into the external adjustment. Without Edran, part of the sequence would remain without a direct explanation.

The clerk called the names. The prosecutor was present, as were Mareval's lawyer, Neria Calden, Amina Dorel, and two representatives of the port authority.

Edran did not appear.

Yuna waited ten minutes before asking whether any communication had arrived.

“No, Magistrate.”

Amiel looked at the empty place. A witness's absence proved nothing about the component or the key. But it changed what they could resolve that day.

***

Mareval's lawyer asked for the hearing to proceed with the available documents.

“The technical inspection has already taken place. Mr. Voss is not the only person who can explain a calibration procedure. There are manuals, records, and other workers.”

The prosecutor objected to treating his absence as irrelevant.

“He was summoned to identify an authorization bearing his signature and explain why he kept a key he was supposed to return. We cannot replace his testimony with an interpretation of the documents.”

Yuna asked Amiel whether he had verified service of the summons.

“It was delivered to his home five days ago. He received it personally and signed the acknowledgment.”

“Was anyone sent to locate him this morning?”

“An officer went to the registered address. The house was locked. The owner said Mr. Voss removed his belongings yesterday.”

The magistrate made a note.

“Is there any indication he was forced to leave?”

“None confirmed.”

“Threats?”

“We have no complaint filed by him. The owner says he left of his own accord.”

The company's lawyer leaned forward.

“Then the witness chose not to appear. The investigation cannot remain suspended indefinitely because of a personal decision.”

Yuna did not answer immediately. She looked at the empty chair, then at the case file.

“It will not remain suspended. But I will not decide an issue that depends on a statement we have not been able to examine.”

***

The prosecutor asked that a note received that morning by Edran's lawyer be read into the record. It was brief and signed.

“I have left Port Cendra of my own free will. I am not being detained, and I do not need anyone to search for me by force. I do not wish to take part in a hearing where my name has already appeared in the newspapers as responsible for the flood. I will leave a contact address when I decide to return. I do not authorize publication of my destination.”

The lawyer confirmed that he had received the note the previous evening. He did not know the destination address and had not been in contact since.

Yuna asked that the note be included under seal, without disclosing personal details beyond what was necessary. Edran's voluntary departure did not automatically release him from his obligations as a witness, but neither did it allow him to be treated as a kidnapped person or a fugitive.

“Are you asking that he be brought here by force?” the magistrate asked the prosecutor.

“Not at this time. I ask that the possibility of summoning him again be preserved and that the authenticity of the note be verified.”

Mareval's lawyer asked that the reference to the absence be removed from the public minutes.

“His absence is a procedural fact,” Yuna said. “His destination and personal reasons are not information the public needs.”

***

Neria asked to speak.

“Without his testimony, can we determine whether the key he presented during the inspection was the same one used in the autumn?”

Amina answered before the magistrate could.

“We can compare the inventory number, geometry, and wear marks. That may indicate compatibility with the lock. It does not establish who used it or when.”

“And the signature on the form?”

“We can compare it with authorized samples. A handwriting examination can identify similarities, but not necessarily who had the document in front of them when it was signed.”

Neria studied the photograph of the outer housing.

“Then we still don't know who carried out the adjustment.”

“We still don't know with certainty,” Amiel said. “We have an order, a key kept outside the register, and a poorly worded form. We don't have a witness confirming the work was performed.”

Mareval's lawyer asked that the record state that the company accepted no inference of responsibility from those elements.

Yuna granted the request. She also stated that Edran's absence neither strengthened nor weakened the hypothesis about the adjustment by itself. The case had to rest on independent evidence.

***

During the recess, Amiel received a message from the officer who had visited the house. The owner confirmed that Edran had left the room keys, paid the outstanding rent, and taken his personal tools. There were no signs of a struggle or damage to the door. A neighbor had seen him get into a hired car with two small suitcases.

The officer had checked the coastal service's boarding register. A passenger with the same name had bought a ticket for the early-morning departure to Distant Bay. The document photograph matched Edran's identity file. The operator could not certify that he had reached his destination, but could confirm that the ticket had been used.

Amiel informed Yuna.

“There are no indications of an involuntary disappearance,” he said. “We have confirmation that he left the city.”

“Are we obliged to follow him?”

“Not to locate him as a missing person. We must, however, preserve the ability to notify him through legal channels if another statement is required.”

The magistrate asked that the information be recorded without turning it into a search order. The case did not need to pursue an adult man who had expressed his wish to leave.

Neria remained silent. She had worked with Edran for years. She did not believe his departure proved guilt, but neither could she ignore that he had chosen to leave before explaining a signature that bore his name.

***

The hearing resumed with the outstanding issue. The prosecutor requested a fifteen-day extension of the preservation order covering the outer housing, the key register, and the calibration forms. She also asked permission to interview two technicians who had worked under Edran's supervision.

Mareval's lawyer accepted preservation of the objects but opposed a general expansion of the inquiry.

Yuna ruled that the components would remain preserved and that the specific interviews could proceed. She made no finding about who had carried out the adjustment or about its connection to the emergency. The issue would remain open for a later hearing if sufficient information emerged or if Edran became available again.

“And the request to close the investigation into the tampering?” the lawyer asked.

“There is no basis to close it or to assign responsibility to a specific person,” Yuna replied. “A witness's absence does not replace the missing evidence.”

The empty chair remained in front of the bench. The clerk removed the glass of water no one had touched.

***

As she left, Neria found Irsa beside the stairs.

“Did he leave?” the operator asked.

“Yes.”

“Do you think he'll come back?”

“I don't know.”

Irsa looked down the corridor.

“He knew who had the keys. If he leaves, it looks as though he's taking the answer with him.”

“The answer doesn't belong to him. The records are still here.”

“But he could explain the signature.”

Neria nodded. She would not pretend his absence did not matter.

In the courtyard, Amiel received a call from the maintenance service. One of the stations had recorded a rise in temperature inside the distribution cabinet, within a range that required monitoring, not evacuation. The technician requested authorization to take measurements during the next shift.

Neria took the note and put it away.

The port could not stop every time an answer was missing. The preservation order remained in force, and work had to continue under stricter limits.

Amiel looked toward the courthouse door.

“The next hearing will have another empty chair if we keep waiting for everyone to return of their own accord.”

“Then we'll have to build the case with what remains,” Neria said.

It was neither a victory nor a defeat. Edran had left by choice, and no one could force him to become the explanation the city wanted.

The hearing had ended without him.

On the table remained the photographs, the key, and the autumn form. Three objects that could be examined again, but could not answer for an absent man.

When the staff closed the room, the chair was set aside against the wall. The case remained open.
