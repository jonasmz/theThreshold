# Echoes of the Threshold
## Season 3 · Episode 16: The Signature at the Water's Edge

**Status:** Narrative draft 0.1. **Point of view:** Daren Holt, limited third person.

***

The man who had signed for the batch wore a coat much too heavy for the season.

Daren Holt watched him stop at the threshold of the statement room, look at the people present, and search for a way out. His name was Bren Tal. He had worked at the former successor secretariat during the reorganization Maelor had approved years earlier. He was not a witness to the Fracture. He had not even been born when it happened.

“I'm not accused of anything, am I?” he asked.

“You've been summoned as a witness to an administrative transfer,” Daren replied. “You have the right to consult your records, request assistance, and correct your statement. I'll explain who will be asking questions.”

Across the table sat a Safety representative authorized to challenge factual claims about records in her agency's custody. An independent secretary would record each turn. The civil preservation proceeding would not be confused with the criminal case Daren was still handling on another matter.

Bren unfolded a piece of paper that had been folded into quarters.

“This is what I signed. I found it among my working copies.”

It was a transfer receipt with two lines of cargo and a date. A stamp certified receipt of a mixed batch. In the corner, someone had added a reference to an “auxiliary ledger.”

Daren requested that the document be identified as a copy submitted by a witness, pending comparison with the original. He did not simply admit it into the evidentiary archive without establishing its chain of custody.

“Did you inspect every book?” he asked.

“I checked that the packages were loaded onto the cart.”

“How many?”

Bren lowered his gaze.

“It says how many there. But I remember three. The paper says two.”

The secretary stopped writing for an instant.

One figure could demolish a memory. It could also show that the receipt described a different stage of the journey.

***

The Safety representative did not wait for Daren to finish.

“Did you sign the dispatch column or the receiving column?”

Bren indicated the first.

“Dispatch. That's why I remember the cart.”

“Then you can't say for certain what reached the other depot.”

“I never said I could.”

Daren kept the questioning within its proper bounds. The challenge was permitted because the witness had consented to this hearing and the authority was entitled to examine the transfer—not because anyone had a license to interrogate him about every restricted archive.

The representative produced a certified weighing ledger: two sealed packages and a small bundle of dividers, entered on a separate line. The record could explain his memory of three items without contradicting the receipt for two document containers.

Bren reached toward the page, but did not touch it.

“That little bundle wasn't tied with the same cord,” he said. “I remember because the binding snapped as we turned onto the ramp.”

“Where did it end up?” Daren asked.

“I think they took it back to the workshop. The paper was damp.”

The representative requested a clarification.

“Did you see it taken back, or did someone tell you?”

Bren took his time.

“I saw them remove it from the cart. I didn't see where they put it.”

Daren had the correction entered into the record. The witness's memory explained a physical separation, not the bundle's destination.

The secretary played back the statement from the beginning. Bren listened to his own voice and asked to amend another sentence: he was not sure the dividers belonged to the auxiliary index. The weighing ledger did not say so either.

“I'm sorry,” he murmured. “The more I talk, the less it seems I know.”

“Knowing where your memory ends is information, too,” Daren said.

Some of the man's tension eased.

***

The change came through a side door.

An assistant entered with a notice from the Safety depot: the sheet marked “restricted” in the digital edition of the receiving ledger was physically bound into the book. The archivist had verified it with an accredited observer without publicly reading its contents. Its page number and the condition of its stitching matched the surrounding leaves.

Daren asked that the image not be displayed. The news was enough to rule out the suspicion that a page absent from the digital edition necessarily meant a page had been physically removed.

Bren straightened.

“So you've found the book.”

“The existence of a sheet has been confirmed,” Daren replied. “Not the identity of the index we're looking for.”

The Safety representative requested inclusion of the physical-inspection record, not the restricted page. The secretary agreed to receive it subject to certification.

Daren then showed the witness a public version of the reorganization orders. One instruction permitted damp packaging to be kept separately from the originals. Another required every item removed from the route to be inventoried.

“Who was supposed to record those movements?” he asked.

Bren recognized the position, not a particular person.

“The conservation watch. If someone took anything off the cart, it had to go into their ledger.”

“Did you check that?”

“No. My job ended at dispatch.”

The representative raised a reasonable objection: there was no basis for assuming the removed bundle contained documents. Daren accepted it. The only supportable next step was to request the watch ledger and look for an entry from the same shift.

Bren closed his eyes.

“I gave my signature to someone who told me it was enough.”

“And perhaps it was enough for your task,” Daren said. “We don't have to turn a limited signature into a guarantee it never offered.”

At the end, the man wanted to take his copy home. Daren arranged for a certified digital copy to be made with his consent and the privately held paper returned to its owner.

***

The hearing had consumed the entire day without producing a confession.

Daren could tell from the expression on Lena Vard's face. She was waiting outside with two others from the neighborhood association. They had hoped the former employee could identify the contents of a missing package. Daren could not share a restricted statement beyond the summary authorized for release.

“Is there anything we can verify?” Lena asked.

“We've identified specific questions for custodians and two interpretations compatible with the records available. There will be public minutes once the authority approves them.”

Lena pressed her lips together.

“The people responsible always have a better word than 'we don't know.'”

Daren thought of the boxes deteriorating while they argued over terminology.

“We can't use that phrase to avoid work. But we can't replace it with a false accusation, either.”

Back at his office, he found a message from the records watch. They had located the ledger for Bren's shift, but only some of its pages were open to consultation. The index described an item entered for “temporary conservation,” with a time and weight but no inventory of contents. The relevant page had passed into a different collection during another reform.

A second custodian. Another procedure. And an object that still had no name.

Daren added the recorded time to the request, along with a specific demand that the originals remain unaltered while access was resolved.

Before closing, he called Bren to tell him he could obtain a copy of his corrected statement. The man answered amid a roomful of voices.

“So was there any point in my coming?”

“Yes. It let us stop asking you about something you couldn't know—and ask the person responsible for the ledger instead.”

Bren was silent.

“I thought a signature would follow me all my life.”

Daren looked at the minutes.

“A signature shouldn't say more than the person actually signed for.”

Rain was beginning outside. Water struck the windows like the cart Bren remembered: a small load, separated from the others, with no proven destination.

The next question was no longer addressed to an old man.

It was addressed to a record someone had been obliged to preserve.

***End of Episode 16.***
