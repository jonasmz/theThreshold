# Echoes of the Threshold
## Season 3 · Episode 09: The Hearing with Two Doors

**Status:** Narrative draft 0.1. **Point of view:** Daren Holt, limited third person.

***

The available room was too small for everyone who had the right to file a claim.

Daren Holt counted the chairs while an assistant checked the recording equipment. A municipal session had been authorized on document preservation and damage to services, with technical agencies invited to respond within their respective jurisdictions. It was not a trial of the Council, nor a criminal hearing in the case Daren was still investigating.

Lena Vard waited beside the door with a folder from the neighborhood association. Beside her stood merchants, a workers' representative, and two spokespeople from communities outside the municipality. Some had traveled since early morning; others needed to give their statements remotely.

“We were told this was a joint meeting,” a merchant protested. “Now you're talking about separating the testimony.”

“Joint doesn't mean one authority acquires the powers of another,” Daren replied. “It means the questions can be heard within the same proceeding, under known limits.”

The presiding authority had requested two segments. The first would review the preservation of and access to municipal records. The second would consider the consequences of restrictions on civilian services, with technical representatives able to explain why certain data were withheld. Objections would be entered in the record, and no one would have to surrender private information to retain the right to make a claim.

Ada Venn had authorized a redacted version of her dining hall's assembly minutes. She had not delegated her voice to Inara or to the inspector. For her remote appearance, her identity would have to be verified privately by the secretary without announcing it to the public.

“If the line doesn't connect,” the assistant asked, “do we represent her statement in writing?”

“Her minutes can be entered for the purpose she authorized. We don't invent oral testimony she never gave.”

In the corridor, someone struck the door with an open palm.

“We're here because nobody listens to us!”

Daren did not answer with a solemn pronouncement. He ordered access opened to an adjoining space where people could hear the session without crowding the emergency exits.

The first responsibility was to make sure no one was hurt before they had a chance to speak.

***

The municipal depot had complied with only part of the preservation order.

Its custodian presented an inventory of the three series placed on hold. Two had been cataloged; the third still mixed originals with reference copies. The presiding authority asked why the examination was incomplete.

“We don't have enough staff to separate pages containing sensitive information,” the man answered. “We can't just hand all the boxes to volunteers.”

Lena rose before she was called.

“The association can pay for cataloging hours.”

Daren requested permission to speak.

“If the association funds the work, it must be clear who controls access and how we prevent the contributor from receiving privileged access to the data.”

The authority agreed to consider an arrangement for administrative support, not direct handling of originals by the parties. It ordered an extension of preservation for a limited period and a cataloging plan with costs and a review of duplicates.

A representative of a historical secretariat explained that the request regarding its annexes had been received, but a security review by another competent authority was still pending. He had brought neither the originals nor a copy of their contents.

“Does that mean the documents exist?” Lena asked.

“It means a series has been identified and is under examination,” the official answered. “The integrity of each item and its conditions of access still need to be verified.”

Daren wrote down the answer. It was neither a confession nor an outright refusal. It gave them grounds to demand a date for the next report without pretending a municipal authority could command an outside agency.

As the first segment ended, a worker asked that his shift records not be destroyed even if his employer ceased operating.

The presiding authority asked for the employment-record custodian to be identified and notified through the appropriate channel. Daren took down the series details, not the man's medical history.

A general petition was beginning to turn into obligations that someone could fulfill and someone else could review.

***

Ada's voice arrived with a delay and a crackle on the line.

“Can you hear me now?”

The secretary confirmed that her identity had been verified through a restricted channel and that Ada consented only to broadcasting her statement about supply costs. The presiding authority gave her the same speaking time as the spokespeople attending in person.

Ada spoke without dramatizing. She listed two transport journeys, their dates, and the debts acknowledged by the dining hall assembly. She separated the cost arising from the closed corridors from the additional expense caused by the bridge's restrictions.

“I'm not here to decide whether a technical installation is safe,” she said. “I'm here because our community's accounts don't appear on the city's forms.”

A Licensing delegate replied that reopening required conditions a civilian hearing could not provide. Daren saw no contradiction between that response and Ada's claim; they addressed different questions.

The workers' representative then requested an aggregate record of canceled rest periods and transport expenses. The municipal office agreed to produce one with personal data protected. A safety adviser offered to explain the general containment criteria in a closed session to accredited observers, but not to publish Anchor configurations.

Then a man in the audience shouted that this was just another kind of concealment.

The presiding authority called a pause. Daren approached the man in the corridor and listened as he described a delivery of medicines that had never reached his neighborhood. He had no receipt, only the carrier's name.

“We'll help you identify the procedure for locating the manifest,” Daren said. “Your account can be entered as a statement, but we won't certify a shipment as lost today without the evidence.”

The man made as though to leave.

“There's always a piece of paper missing.”

“Sometimes it's missing because someone failed to preserve it. That's exactly why we're here.”

Daren returned to the room after the hearing had resumed.

***

The final ruling was narrower than the crowd had expected.

Preservation of the municipal series was extended, an access plan with data protections was required, and a channel for claims from outside the municipality was opened. Each department remained responsible for its own documents. The technical body agreed to consider an audit involving observers, provided the proposal defined risks, powers, and funding.

No authority lifted the prohibition on the corridors or made a finding of historical responsibility.

Daren read the minutes before signing the acknowledgment of receipt. He refused to let one line describe the historical official's appearance as “delivery of conclusive evidence.” The secretary replaced those words with “appearance of custodian and report on location and access restrictions.” The official, who was still present, accepted the change.

Lena approached him when it was over.

“We got another hearing, but we didn't get an answer.”

“You got named recipients and deadlines for your questions,” Daren said. “Any answer they give will have to stand up to review.”

“And if it never comes?”

Daren remembered how many years a case file could remain open without its existence being enough to repair anyone's life.

“Then there will be a record of who failed to comply. And you'll be able to challenge the omission through the proper channels.”

In the lobby, Ada had sent a note to the secretary: her connection had dropped before she heard the ending. She requested a copy of the minutes and the decision's scope regarding claimants outside the municipality.

Daren made sure it was sent to her authorized contact. He did not mistake that for a victory.

Before leaving, he received the technical proposal for the next stage. It called for a shared audit of safety criteria, decision records, and civilian consequences, but left a blank space for the names of independent observers.

The document was six pages long.

Its most important field still contained no one.

***End of Episode 09.***
